## Abstract

This outlook tracks the Index of Consumer Spending across discretionary and
non-discretionary merchant categories for the first half of 2026. Headline growth in
the Index of Consumer Spending decelerated to 1.4 per cent quarter over quarter, with
durable goods contracting 2.3 per cent while services held flat.

## Key Findings

- Households in the lower two income quintiles shifted an additional 5 per cent of
  monthly outlays toward grocery and utilities, crowding out discretionary retail.
- Buy-now-pay-later transaction volumes rose 19 per cent year over year, signaling
  liquidity stress beneath stable headline spending.
- Merchant pattern data show restaurant traffic diverging sharply between premium and
  quick-service segments, a classic late-cycle bifurcation.

## Outlook

Absent a rate cut, we project the Index of Consumer Spending to remain range-bound
through the third quarter, with downside risk concentrated in big-ticket durables.
