## Abstract

This study examines how the deployment of agentic AI systems and enterprise AI
assistants is reshaping demand for entry-level knowledge work across North American
firms. Using payroll panel data from 2024 through 2026, we estimate that occupations
with high task-automation exposure saw posted vacancies contract by roughly 18 per cent,
while adjacent supervisory and AI-orchestration roles expanded by 11 per cent.

## Key Findings

- Displacement concentrates in junior analyst, paralegal-support, and tier-one customer
  service roles; senior judgment-intensive roles remain insulated.
- Firms adopting AI agents for task automation report compressed hiring funnels rather
  than mass layoffs: attrition backfills are quietly suspended.
- Regional effects are uneven, with Canadian metropolitan markets lagging United States
  adoption by approximately three quarters.

## Policy Implications

Retraining subsidies tied to AI-orchestration competencies outperform broad wage
supports in our simulations by a wide margin, improving re-employment odds by
24 per cent within twelve months.
